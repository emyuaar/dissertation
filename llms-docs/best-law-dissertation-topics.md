## Best Law Dissertation Topics for 2026 & 2027: Ideas for LLB and LLM Students

Choosing a law dissertation topic is a peculiar kind of freedom.

For most of your degree, someone else asks the question. A problem appears on an examination paper or seminar sheet, and your task is to answer it. Then the dissertation arrives and, rather inconveniently, asks you to decide what the problem should be.

That is often harder.

The best law dissertation topics for **2026 and 2027** are not necessarily the newest or most dramatic legal controversies. They are subjects where something remains unsettled: legislation has changed, courts disagree, technology has outrun old rules, reform is under consideration, or a familiar principle produces consequences worth questioning.

This makes areas such as **artificial intelligence, data protection, employment rights, digital assets, online safety, criminal-law reform, housing, assisted dying and family-law reform** particularly interesting for current LLB and LLM research.

But a good dissertation needs more than a fashionable subject.

It needs a question.

### Quick List: 12 Strong Law Dissertation Topics for 2026 & 2027

If you want ideas immediately, these are among the strongest contemporary areas to investigate:

- **AI training and copyright infringement under UK law**

- **Automated decision-making after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025**

- **Whether UK law adequately protects people from AI discrimination**

- **Digital assets as personal property after the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025**

- **The Employment Rights Act 2025 and the future of unfair dismissal**

- **Legal protection for zero-hours workers**

- **Children's protection under the Online Safety Act 2023**

- **Whether the law of homicide in England and Wales requires fundamental reform**

- **Reforming criminal appeals and correcting miscarriages of justice**

- **Whether financial-remedy law on divorce produces sufficiently consistent outcomes**

- **The abolition of Section 21 and the changing balance between landlords and tenants**

- **Whether proposed assisted-dying legislation contains sufficient safeguards**

Several of these areas are undergoing active statutory or policy development in 2026. The Employment Rights Act 2025 is being implemented in stages into 2027; major provisions of the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 came into force during 2026; and the Law Commission has current projects covering homicide, criminal appeals, commercial leasehold, kinship care and other areas.

That makes them timely.

It does not automatically make them easy.

## What Makes a Good Dissertation Topics in Law?

A good dissertation topic sits somewhere between **“too obvious”** and **“impossible to finish before graduation”**.

“Artificial Intelligence and Law” is too broad.

“The compatibility of one provision governing automated decision-making with a particular legal principle” may be manageable.

Before choosing a title, ask five questions.

### Is There a Real Legal Problem?

A subject is not yet a dissertation question.

**Employment law** is a subject.

**Whether the reduction in the unfair-dismissal qualifying period properly balances job security against employers' need for flexibility** is a research problem.

The second gives you something to argue.

### Is There Enough Primary Law?

Check whether you can find:

- legislation;

- cases;

- statutory instruments;

- consultation material;

- parliamentary documents;

- regulatory guidance; or

- international instruments.

A brilliant-sounding title becomes frustrating when there is almost nothing authoritative to analyse.

### Is There Enough Academic Debate?

A dissertation should enter a conversation rather than deliver a lecture to an empty room.

Look for:

- journal articles;

- books;

- case notes;

- competing theories;

- law-reform papers; and

- credible policy analysis.

### Is the Question Narrow Enough?

A dissertation has a word limit.
The law does not.
The narrower question usually wins.

If narrowing the subject is proving difficult, start with our guide to [choosing a strong dissertation topic](https://onlinedissertationadvisors.co.uk/how-to-choose-a-strong-dissertation-topic/).

### Can You Evaluate It?

Questions beginning with **“What is...?”** frequently lead towards description.

Questions beginning with:

- To what extent...

- How effective...

- Should...

- Does the current law adequately...

- Is reform necessary...

- Has the law successfully...

usually create more room for analysis. 
Now to the ideas themselves.

## Artificial Intelligence and Technology Law Dissertation Topics

Few areas of law are moving as quickly as artificial intelligence.

That creates opportunity, but also danger. An AI dissertation becomes weak very quickly when it spends half its word count explaining what ChatGPT is.

The legal problem must remain central.

### 1. Is UK Copyright Law Fit for Generative AI Training?

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent does the existing UK copyright framework provide a fair balance between rights holders and developers training generative AI models?**

This is one of the strongest 2026–27 intellectual-property topics.

The UK Government published its report and impact assessment on copyright and AI in March 2026 after consulting on questions including licensing, transparency, text and data mining, enforcement and AI-generated outputs. The Government stated that it would not introduce copyright reforms until satisfied that they met its objectives.

Possible angles include:

- text and data mining;

- licensing;

- training data;

- creator remuneration;

- transparency;

- infringement;

- AI-generated outputs; and

- the position of creative industries.

### 2. Who Should Be Liable When Generative AI Produces Infringing Content?

#### Possible research question:

**Does UK copyright law provide sufficient certainty when generative AI outputs reproduce protected expression?**

This is narrower than writing generally about “AI and copyright”.

You could examine:

- substantial-part infringement;

- responsibility of developers;

- responsibility of users;

- intermediary liability;

- evidential difficulties; and

- whether existing principles remain workable when output is generated probabilistically.

### 3. Automated Decision-Making After the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent has the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 created an appropriate legal framework for automated decision-making involving personal data?**

The Act amended parts of UK data-protection law, including the rules surrounding automated decision-making. Most of its principal data-protection and privacy changes commenced on **5 February 2026**.

That makes this considerably fresher than another generic dissertation entitled *The Effectiveness of the GDPR*.

### 4. AI Discrimination in Recruitment

#### Possible research question:

**Does UK equality law adequately protect job applicants from discriminatory decisions made or influenced by AI recruitment systems?**

Possible areas:

- Equality Act 2010;

- indirect discrimination;

- evidential difficulties;

- transparency;

- algorithmic bias;

- automated decision-making;

- employer responsibility; and

- intersectional discrimination.

This is particularly good for someone interested in both employment and technology law.

### 5. Facial Recognition and Article 8 Privacy Rights

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent does the legal framework governing live facial recognition by police adequately protect Article 8 rights in England and Wales?**

Possible areas:

- privacy;

- proportionality;

- police powers;

- biometric data;

- discrimination;

- surveillance;

- judicial review; and

- data protection.

A strong dissertation would avoid becoming a general essay on surveillance and concentrate on a particular use or authority.

### 6. Should AI Systems Be Subject to a Dedicated UK Liability Regime?

#### Possible research question:

**Are existing principles of negligence and product liability capable of addressing harm caused by autonomous AI systems?**

This could become especially interesting alongside the Law Commission's current **product liability** review.

An LLM dissertation might compare the UK approach with another jurisdiction.

### 7. Deepfakes, Consent and Intimate Images

#### Possible research question:

**Does the current criminal-law framework adequately address AI-generated intimate images created without consent?**

The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 introduced provisions relating to creating or requesting purported intimate images of adults without consent, with relevant offences brought into force in February 2026.

This creates fertile ground for examining:

- consent;

- synthetic media;

- sexual autonomy;

- criminalisation;

- mens rea;

- freedom of expression; and

- enforcement.

### 8. Are Digital Assets Really Property?

#### Possible research question:

**What are the implications of the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 for the traditional categories of personal property under English law?**

The Act received Royal Assent on **2 December 2025** and provides that a digital or electronic thing is not prevented from being an object of personal-property rights merely because it falls outside the traditional categories of things in possession and things in action.

This is an excellent private-law topic for 2026–27.

Possible subjects include:

- cryptoassets;

- property classification;

- insolvency;

- trusts;

- theft;

- tracing; and

- proprietary remedies.

#### Employment Law Dissertation Topics

Employment law is unusually rich for 2026 and 2027 because the **Employment Rights Act 2025** introduced extensive reforms, many of which are being implemented in stages.

### 9. The New Six-Month Unfair-Dismissal Qualifying Period

#### Possible research question:

**Does reducing the qualifying period for ordinary unfair-dismissal protection from two years to six months strike a fair balance between job security and managerial flexibility?**

The Government intends the change to operate from **1 January 2027**.

This is almost tailor-made for a 2026–27 employment-law dissertation.

### 10. Removing the Cap on Unfair-Dismissal Compensation

#### Possible research question:

**Is the removal of the compensatory award cap for unfair dismissal justified in principle and likely to improve access to effective remedies?**

The Employment Rights Act 2025 removes the existing cap on compensatory awards for successful unfair-dismissal claims.

Possible arguments:

- compensation;

- deterrence;

- proportionality;

- employer uncertainty;

- access to justice; and

- tribunal litigation.

### 11. Are Zero-Hours Workers Adequately Protected?

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent do the guaranteed-hours provisions of the Employment Rights Act 2025 address the insecurity associated with zero-hours work?**

The Act introduces rights relating to guaranteed hours, reasonable notice of shifts and payments where qualifying shifts are cancelled, moved or curtailed at short notice, that creates much more substance than the old general question:

> “Are zero-hours contracts fair?”

### 12. Fire and Rehire After the Employment Rights Act 2025

#### Possible research question:

**Has the Employment Rights Act 2025 achieved an appropriate balance between preventing abusive fire-and-rehire practices and preserving legitimate business restructuring?**

A good dissertation could evaluate:

- dismissal;

- contractual variation;

- employer justification;

- remedies;

- collective consultation; and

- business necessity.

### 13. Flexible Working and Employer Reasonableness

#### Possible research question:

**Does the developing statutory framework for flexible working place an appropriate burden on employers?**

This could include:

- home working;

- hybrid working;

- reasonableness;

- discrimination;

- caring responsibilities; and

- changing post-pandemic workplace expectations.

### 14. Worker Status in the Platform Economy

#### Possible research question:

**Does the employee-worker-self-employed distinction remain adequate for platform and gig-economy work?**

Uber and other platform cases have already produced substantial scholarship.

The way to make this topic worthwhile now is to narrow it:

- delivery drivers;

- algorithmic management;

- substitution clauses;

- control;

- working time; or

- one particular platform model.

#### Criminal Law Dissertation Topics

Criminal law offers several unusually current reform questions.

The Law Commission is presently reviewing **homicide** and **criminal appeals**, while its latest reform programme also contains planned work on consent and the defence of insanity.

### 15. Does the Law of Murder Need Fundamental Reform?

#### Possible research question:

**Is the distinction between murder and manslaughter conceptually coherent under the current law of England and Wales?**

The Law Commission is carrying out a major review of homicide offences, including full and partial defences and the sentencing framework for murder.

A dissertation could focus specifically on:

- intention;

- murder;

- manslaughter;

- partial defences; or

- mandatory life sentences.

Do not attempt all of homicide law in 10,000 words.

### 16. Should the Mandatory Life Sentence for Murder Be Reformed?

#### Possible research question:

**Is the mandatory life sentence for murder capable of producing proportionate sentencing across materially different forms of homicide?**

This gives you:

- sentencing theory;

- proportionality;

- judicial discretion;

- culpability;

- comparative law; and

- current reform material.

### 17. Loss of Control as a Partial Defence

#### Possible research question:

**Does the loss-of-control defence provide a coherent and fair response to reduced culpability in homicide cases?**

Possible focus:

- domestic abuse;

- fear;

- anger;

- sexual infidelity;

- gendered application; and

- comparison with the former provocation defence.

### 18. Reforming Criminal Appeals

#### Possible research question:

**Does the current 'safety' test provide an adequate mechanism for correcting miscarriages of justice?**

The Law Commission's criminal-appeals work is examining appeal tests, the Criminal Cases Review Commission, evidence retention and access, compensation and support for wrongly convicted people.

This is an excellent dissertation for someone interested in criminal justice rather than substantive offences.

### 19. Should the Criminal Cases Review Commission Be Reformed?

#### Possible research question:

**Is the relationship between the Criminal Cases Review Commission and the Court of Appeal capable of correcting miscarriages of justice effectively?**

This is narrower and probably easier to control than “reform criminal appeals”.

### 20. Consent in Criminal Law

#### Possible research question:

**Does English criminal law apply a sufficiently coherent concept of consent across different offences?**

The Law Commission's 14th Programme includes a forthcoming project reviewing consent in criminal law with the aim of improving predictability and modernising the law.

A narrower dissertation might concentrate on:

- sexual offences;

- bodily harm;

- deception;

- intoxication; or

- consent to medical treatment.

### 21. Should the Defence of Insanity Be Modernised?

#### Possible research question:

**Is the M'Naghten framework for the insanity defence compatible with modern understandings of mental disorder and criminal responsibility?**

The defence of insanity is another subject within the Law Commission's current reform programme.

This gives an old doctrine a very current reason for re-examination.

#### Human Rights and Public Law Dissertation Topics

Human-rights dissertations become weak when they simply ask whether rights are “important”.

Choose a conflict.

Rights become legally interesting when two legitimate interests pull in different directions.

### 22. Online Safety vs Freedom of Expression

#### Possible research question:

**Does the Online Safety Act framework achieve a proportionate balance between protection from online harm and freedom of expression?**

Ofcom continues to publish and update regulatory material under the Online Safety Act, including risk assessments concerning illegal harms and content harmful to children.

Possible angles:

- age assurance;

- children's safety;

- platform duties;

- lawful expression;

- privacy;

- encrypted services; or

- proportionality.

### 23. Children's Rights Under the Online Safety Regime

#### Possible research question:

**Are the Online Safety Act's protections for children sufficiently effective without imposing disproportionate restrictions on privacy and expression?**

This is narrower and often stronger than attempting the whole Act.

### 24. Accessibility of Public Transport and Disability Rights

#### Possible research question:

**Does the existing legal framework provide effective and enforceable transport accessibility rights for disabled people in England and Wales?**

The Law Commission began a review of transport accessibility law in 2026.

That supplies a strong contemporary reform context.

### 25. Government Surveillance and Proportionality

#### Possible research question:

**Does the UK's legal framework for digital surveillance provide sufficient safeguards against disproportionate interference with privacy?**

You could narrow this to:

- communications data;

- facial recognition;

- bulk powers;

- police surveillance; or

- national security.

### 26. Economic Sanctions and Human Rights

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent should Human Rights Act protections apply to overseas entities subjected to UK financial sanctions?**

This is more specialised and therefore particularly suitable for an LLM dissertation.

#### Family Law Dissertation Topics

Family law remains one of the richest subjects for evaluation because fairness is frequently easier to describe than to define.

### 27. Financial Remedies on Divorce

#### Possible research question:

**Does the discretionary framework governing financial remedies on divorce provide sufficient certainty and consistency?**

The Law Commission concluded in its recent work that financial-remedies law requires reform, highlighting issues including judicial discretion, maintenance, pensions and the treatment of conduct.

This is one of the best family-law topics available right now.

### 28. Should England and Wales Introduce Clearer Rules for Dividing Matrimonial Property?

#### Possible research question:

**Would a more rules-based approach to matrimonial property produce fairer and more predictable divorce outcomes than the present discretionary system?**

This offers excellent comparative-law potential.

You could compare England and Wales with:

- Scotland;

- another common-law jurisdiction; or

- a European system.

### 29. Pensions in Divorce Settlements

#### Possible research question:

**Does the law adequately protect financially weaker spouses where pension assets form a substantial part of matrimonial wealth?**

This is narrower, practical and socially important.

### 30. Kinship Care Reform

#### Possible research question:

**Does the legal framework governing kinship care adequately recognise and support relatives who assume responsibility for children?**

The Law Commission published a kinship-care consultation paper in **July 2026** as part of its ongoing reform project.

Very current.

### 31. Surrogacy Law Reform

#### Possible research question:

**Does the current legal framework for surrogacy in England and Wales adequately protect intended parents, surrogates and children?**

The Law Commission's earlier surrogacy reform project remains awaiting implementation.

Potential issues:

- legal parenthood;

- parental orders;

- consent;

- commercialisation;

- cross-border arrangements; and

- welfare of the child.

#### Medical Law Dissertation Topics

Medical law gives you some of the hardest legal questions because autonomy, protection, ethics and state power frequently collide.

### 32. Assisted Dying in England and Wales

#### Possible research question:

**Would the safeguards proposed for assisted dying adequately protect autonomy while preventing coercion and abuse?**

This is extremely current.

The earlier Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill failed to complete its parliamentary stages before the end of the 2024–26 session. A new **Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill 2026–27** was introduced in June 2026, with its second reading scheduled for **11 September 2026**.

Because its legislative status may change while a dissertation is being written, the title should be framed carefully.

Do not write:

> “The new assisted-dying law…”

unless it has actually become law.

### 33. Assisted Dying and Capacity

#### Possible research question:

**Is mental-capacity assessment capable of providing a sufficiently reliable safeguard within an assisted-dying framework?**

This is much narrower than evaluating assisted dying generally.

Possible issues:

- voluntariness;

- coercion;

- capacity;

- depression;

- disability rights; and

- professional judgement.

### 34. Medical Consent and Artificial Intelligence

#### Possible research question:

**Can patients give legally meaningful informed consent when diagnosis or treatment recommendations depend substantially on artificial intelligence?**

A good interdisciplinary topic involving:

- consent;

- explainability;

- negligence;

- clinician responsibility;

- AI; and

- patient autonomy.

#### Property and Housing Law Dissertation Topics

Housing law changed considerably in 2026, making it a much more interesting dissertation field than it may first appear.

### 35. Abolition of Section 21 No-Fault Evictions

#### Possible research question:

**Has the abolition of Section 21 achieved a fairer balance between security of tenure and landlords' property interests?**

Major provisions of the Renters' Rights reforms took effect on **1 May 2026**, including the end of Section 21 no-fault evictions and a move away from fixed-term tenancies in the private rented sector.

This is a particularly strong LLB topic because the legal change is concrete and recent.

### 36. Rent Regulation and Landlord Autonomy

#### Possible research question:

**Do the new restrictions governing rent increases provide tenants with adequate protection without unduly interfering with landlords' economic interests?**

This allows a more focused examination of one part of the new tenancy regime.

### 37. Commercial Leasehold Reform

#### Possible research question:

**Does the existing commercial leasehold framework create unnecessary barriers to efficient transactions and business tenancy renewal?**

The Law Commission is actively reviewing commercial leasehold law and published a consultation paper in June 2026.

An excellent specialist property-law topic.

### 38. Chancel Repair Liability

#### Possible research question:

**Should chancel repair liability continue to bind modern property owners in England and Wales?**

Yes, the medieval-sounding problem still exists.

That is precisely what makes it interesting.

The Law Commission is currently examining reform intended to address remaining problems with chancel repair liability and registration.

A good dissertation does not always need robots.

Sometimes an ancient legal relic produces the better question.

#### Commercial and Company Law Dissertation Topics

Commercial law students have several strong current reform opportunities.

### 39. Arbitration After the Arbitration Act 2025

#### Possible research question:

**Has the Arbitration Act 2025 strengthened England and Wales as a competitive jurisdiction for international commercial arbitration?**

The Arbitration Act 2025 received Royal Assent on **24 February 2025** and amended the Arbitration Act 1996. Among its reforms is a statutory rule concerning the law applicable to an arbitration agreement.

Possible angles:

- governing law;

- arbitrator duties;

- summary disposal;

- court intervention;

- jurisdictional challenges; and

- international competitiveness.

### 40. Digital Assets and Private International Law

#### Possible research question:

**Can traditional choice-of-law rules provide sufficient certainty for disputes involving decentralised digital assets?**

The Law Commission has an active project examining digital assets and electronic trade documents in private international law.

This would make an ambitious but excellent LLM topic.

### 41. Consumer Class Actions

#### Possible research question:

**Should England and Wales introduce a broader consumer class-action regime?**

The Law Commission announced a project in 2026 considering the introduction of such a regime.

Possible issues:

- collective redress;

- litigation funding;

- access to justice;

- defendant protection;

- opt-in vs opt-out proceedings; and

- comparative approaches.

### 42. Product Liability for Software and AI

#### Possible research question:

**Should software and AI systems be treated as products for the purposes of strict product liability?**

This combines technology and commercial law while remaining focused on a traditional private-law question.

It is also timely because product liability is under active Law Commission review.

#### Environmental Law Dissertation Topics

Environmental dissertations work best when the legal mechanism is precise.

“Climate change law” is not a dissertation question.

### 43. Environmental Regulation of Farming

#### Possible research question:

The Law Commission began a review of environmental legislation applying to agricultural businesses in England in 2026.

### 44. Corporate Climate Responsibility

#### Possible research question:

**To what extent should company directors be required to take climate-related interests into account when fulfilling their statutory duties?**

This can connect:

- Companies Act 2006;

- directors' duties;

- shareholder primacy;

- stakeholder interests;

- ESG;

- disclosure; and

- greenwashing.

Avoid turning it into a general environmental-policy essay.

Keep the company-law question central.

#### International Law Dissertation Topics

International law topics can become enormous very quickly.

The safest approach is to choose one doctrine and one problem.

### 45. Economic Sanctions and International Legal Responsibility

#### Possible research question:

**Do modern financial-sanctions regimes provide adequate procedural protection for designated individuals and entities?**

Possible sources:

- sanctions legislation;

- judicial review;

- human rights;

- due process;

- property rights; and

- national-security justifications.

### 46. Autonomous Weapons and International Humanitarian Law

#### Possible research question:

**Can existing principles of distinction and proportionality adequately govern autonomous weapons systems?**

The technology may be new.

The better dissertation question is whether old legal principles remain capable of governing it.

### 47. State Responsibility for Cyber Operations

#### Possible research question:

**When should a state be held internationally responsible for a cyber operation carried out by non-state actors from its territory?**

This offers strong doctrinal material around:

- attribution;

- due diligence;

- sovereignty;

- countermeasures; and

- state responsibility.

### How to Turn One of These Ideas Into Your Own Dissertation Topic

Do not copy a title simply because it appears attractive.

Improve it.

Suppose you begin with:

> **AI and Employment Law**

Too broad.

Narrow it:

> **AI Discrimination in Recruitment**

Better.

Then identify the jurisdiction:

> **AI Discrimination in Recruitment Under UK Employment Law**

Better again.

Then turn it into a question:

> **To What Extent Does the Equality Act 2010 Protect Job Applicants from Discriminatory AI Recruitment Systems?**

Now you have something capable of producing a dissertation.

The same process works elsewhere:

**Broad:**
Criminal law

**Narrower:**
Homicide

**Better:**
Loss of control

**Research question:**
*Does the loss-of-control defence provide an adequate legal response to victims of prolonged domestic abuse who kill their abusers?*

The topic becomes stronger each time something unnecessary is removed.

## Which Law Dissertation Topics Are Best for LLB Students?

LLB dissertations usually benefit from a question with:

- a clear jurisdiction;

- established primary law;

- a manageable doctrinal problem;

- sufficient academic commentary; and

- limited methodological complexity.

Strong examples from this list include:

- Section 21 abolition;

- unfair-dismissal reform;

- zero-hours worker protection;

- loss of control;

- financial remedies on divorce;

- online safety and freedom of expression;

- automated decision-making;

- data protection; and

- digital assets as property.

You do not need to choose a boring subject.

You need a subject you can control.

### Which Topics Are Better for LLM Students?

LLM work often allows greater theoretical, comparative or specialist depth.

Good possibilities include:

- AI copyright licensing;

- digital assets and private international law;

- consumer class actions;

- arbitration after the Arbitration Act 2025;

- international responsibility for cyber operations;

- autonomous weapons;

- product liability for AI;

- comparative assisted-dying regulation;

- financial-remedy reform across jurisdictions; and

- algorithmic discrimination.

These topics can support more sophisticated comparative or theoretical analysis.

But an LLM title should not become broad merely because the degree is postgraduate.

Depth still beats size.

### Should You Choose a Very New Law Dissertation Topic?

Not always.

Novelty can be seductive.

A law passed last week may seem like the perfect dissertation subject until you discover that:

- almost no cases exist;

- very little academic literature has been published;

- regulations are still missing;

- commencement is incomplete; or

- the legal position changes halfway through your writing.

Sometimes the better dissertation examines an older doctrine in light of a new development.

For example:

Instead of:

> **The Employment Rights Act 2025**

consider:

> **Does the six-month qualifying period introduced by the Employment Rights Act 2025 create a more principled approach to unfair-dismissal protection?**

The new legislation gives the dissertation relevance.

The established doctrine gives it depth.

## How Many Law Dissertation Topics Should You Consider Before Choosing?

Three to five serious possibilities are usually more useful than collecting fifty titles.

For each possible topic, carry out a short feasibility test.

Ask:

### Can I find the primary law?

If not, stop.

### Can I find recent scholarship?

If not, think carefully.

### Can I identify a disagreement?

If everyone agrees, what exactly will you evaluate?

### Can I explain the question in one sentence?

If not, it may still be too broad.

### Could the law change while I am writing?

If yes, can the dissertation accommodate that?

Once you have a promising question, the next step is usually a proposal. Our guide explaining [what a dissertation proposal is](https://onlinedissertationadvisors.co.uk/what-is-a-dissertation-proposal-complete-uk-guide-for-students/) can help you move from an interesting idea towards an actual research plan.

## Do You Need Primary Research for a Law Dissertation?

Usually not if the project is doctrinal.

A doctrinal dissertation may be built from:

- legislation;

- cases;

- regulatory material;

- official reports; and

- academic scholarship.

Other projects may use:

- comparative research;

- socio-legal methods;

- interviews;

- surveys; or

- empirical evidence.

The method should follow the question.

Do not add interviews merely because they appear sophisticated.

If your research question asks how courts have interpreted legislation, interviewing twenty people will not necessarily help you answer it.

Our broader guide to [law dissertation writing](https://onlinedissertationadvisors.co.uk/how-to-write-a-law-dissertation-a-complete-uk-guide-for-llb-llm-students/) explains doctrinal, comparative and socio-legal approaches in more detail.

## Where Can You Find Sources for a Law Dissertation Topic?

Begin with authority rather than commentary.

Depending on the subject, useful starting points include:

- legislation.gov.uk;

- UK Supreme Court judgments;

- Court of Appeal decisions;

- GOV.UK;

- UK Parliament;

- Law Commission publications;

- Westlaw UK;

- Lexis+ UK;

- HeinOnline;

- university library databases; and

- specialist legal journals.

Then move into academic debate.

If you begin entirely with random web articles, there is a danger that someone else's simplification becomes the foundation of your research.

A dissertation deserves a firmer floor.

## Final Thoughts

There is no perfect dissertation topic waiting somewhere to be discovered.

There is only a promising question and the work required to sharpen it.

The best law dissertation topics for 2026 and 2027 are attractive because the law around them is moving. Employment rights are changing. Parliament is again considering assisted dying. Data law has been amended. Digital assets have acquired clearer recognition within property law. Artificial intelligence continues to test copyright, discrimination and liability. The Law Commission is examining old doctrines that no longer sit comfortably in modern life.

But fashion alone will not write the dissertation.

Choose a problem narrow enough to understand. Find the strongest authority you can. Read people who disagree with one another. Ask what the law is trying to achieve, whether it succeeds and what would happen if it changed.

A fashionable title may catch attention.

A precise question produces the better dissertation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1. What are the best law dissertation topics for 2026 and 2027?

Strong current areas include AI and copyright, automated decision-making, digital assets, employment-rights reform, zero-hours work, online safety, homicide reform, criminal appeals, financial remedies on divorce, renters' rights and assisted dying. The best individual topic is one narrow enough to research critically within your university's word limit.

### Q2. What is a good LLB dissertation topic?

A good LLB topic normally has a clear legal problem, accessible primary authorities and enough academic scholarship for critical analysis. Employment law, criminal law, family law, human rights, technology law and property law all provide strong possibilities.

### Q3. What is a good LLM dissertation topic?

LLM topics can support more specialised, comparative or theoretical work. AI copyright, digital assets, arbitration, international cyber law, algorithmic discrimination and comparative medical-law questions can all provide suitable postgraduate research areas.

### Q4. Are AI law dissertation topics a good choice?

They can be excellent if the legal question is precise. “Artificial Intelligence and Law” is far too broad. A question about AI training and copyright, algorithmic discrimination or liability for AI-generated harm is considerably stronger.

### Q5. How do I make my law dissertation topic original?

Originality does not necessarily require discovering a legal issue nobody has ever considered. You can create useful originality by applying an established doctrine to a new development, comparing competing approaches, examining recent legislation or challenging an accepted interpretation.

### Q6. Should I choose a topic based on recent legislation?

Recent legislation can make an excellent dissertation subject, but check whether enough commentary and supporting authority exist. If implementation is still changing, frame the research question so that the dissertation does not become obsolete after one statutory instrument or parliamentary vote.

### Q7. Can I change my law dissertation topic after starting?

University rules vary, but topic refinement is normal during early research. A broad topic often becomes narrower after preliminary reading. Major changes should be discussed with your supervisor, particularly after a proposal has been formally approved.

### Q8. How narrow should a law dissertation topic be?

Narrow enough that you can answer the question properly rather than surveying an entire field. “Human rights law” is too broad. “Whether live facial recognition by police provides adequate protection for Article 8 privacy rights” is much more manageable.

### Q9. Should a law dissertation topic be a question?

It does not always have to appear grammatically as a question in the final title, but the project should have a clear research question behind it. Without one, the dissertation can easily become descriptive.
